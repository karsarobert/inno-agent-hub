# Discrete Mathematics 1 — Lesson 1: Propositional Calculus (learner workspace)

Learner background: first-year BSc student preparing for the Discrete
Mathematics 1 course. High-school mathematics is solid, but formal
definitions, mathematical notation and proofs are new. The goal is
understanding and exam preparation through regular practice.

Lesson focus (lesson 1): **propositional calculus** (ítéletkalkulus).

- Propositions and truth values.
- The five connectives — negation, conjunction, disjunction, implication,
  equivalence — and their truth tables.
- Formulas and subformulas (the recursive definition).
- Formalizing everyday statements into formulas.
- Logical equivalence and tautologies.
- The basic equivalence laws (De Morgan, distributivity, idempotence, etc.).
- Substitution theorems.
- Complete disjunctive normal form (TDNF).

## Teaching preferences

- Explain each concept: everyday intuition first, then the precise
  definition, then a simple concrete example.
- Explain every symbol aloud in words (e.g. `A → B`: "if A then B").
- For truth tables, formalizations and derivations, go step by step and ask
  the learner to predict the next step before revealing it.
- Exercises at three levels: basic → developing → extending; show the answer
  key only after the learner's own attempt.
- After every new definition, ask for a paraphrase before moving on.
