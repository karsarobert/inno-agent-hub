Module 1 • Ioffe & Szegedy (2015) Internal Covariate Shift

Batch Normalization: Stabilizing & Accelerating Deep Neural Networks

Neural networks are trained on Mini-Batches (here m = 6 samples at once). When these 6 samples enter a single hidden neuron, that neuron computes 6 weighted sums: these 6 values are x₁, x₂, …, x₆! Batch Normalization standardizes these 6 values (μ = 0, σ = 1) before they enter the non-linear activation function.

Neural Network Topology & Neuron X-Ray

Inside Neuron N₁: Weighted Sum (x₁..x₆) → Batch Norm (y₁..y₆) → Activation

Mini-Batch Size: m = 6 samples
1. What are x₁ … x₆? They are the pre-activation weighted sums (z = W · in + b) inside a single neuron across the 6 samples in the current mini-batch!
2. Why 6 values? Because our Mini-Batch size is m = 6. If the batch size were 32, this single neuron would process x₁ … x₃₂ simultaneously.
3. Where is Batch Norm placed? Directly after the linear weighted sum (xᵢ) and before the non-linear activation function (f(yᵢ)).
Mini-Batch (Rows) vs. Neurons (Columns)

How does Batch Norm compute stats? COLUMN-WISE!

1 Row = 1 data sample (house) in the batch. 1 Column = 1 neuron's activations across all 6 samples. Batch Norm computes mean (μB) and std (σB) vertically per neuron!

Module 2 • He et al. (2015) — CVPR Best Paper

Skip Connections & Residual Networks (ResNet): Training 152+ Layer Networks

Instead of forcing stacked layers to learn an underlying mapping H(x) from scratch, a Skip Connection (Identity Shortcut) routes the input x directly to the output addition node, so weighted layers only need to learn the residual difference: F(x) = H(x) − x!

x (Identity) + F(x) (Residual) = H(x) (Output)

2.1 The Degradation Paradox (He et al., 2015 — Figure 1 CIFAR-10)

Why did a 56-layer Plain Network perform worse than a 20-layer network on both training and test data?

Plain Network: 56-Layer has HIGHER error than 20-Layer!

20-Layer (Train Error) 6.1%
56-Layer (Train Error) 10.8% (Worse!)
20-Layer (Test Error) 8.7%
56-Layer (Test Error) 13.4% (Worse!)

Teacher's Note: Why is this NOT Overfitting?

⚠️ Crucial Exam Distinction:

If this were overfitting, the 56-layer training error (dashed curve) would drop near 0% while only test error rose. Here, training error itself is much higher! This proves it is an optimization difficulty (Degradation Problem).

🧠 He et al.'s Thought Experiment:

Take a trained 20-layer network and stack 36 Identity Mapping (H(x) = x) layers on top. In theory, it should have no higher error than the 20-layer net! Standard nonlinear layers struggle to approximate H(x) = x, whereas with H(x) = F(x) + x, the solver simply drives weights toward zero (F(x) = 0).

Module 3 • Srivastava et al. (2014) & Weight Decay

Regularization Masterclass: Fighting Overfitting with Dropout & L2 Penalty

High-capacity neural networks easily memorize random training noise (Overfitting). We combat this via Dropout (randomly deactivating neurons during training to prevent co-adaptation) and L2 Regularization / Weight Decay (penalizing large weights in the loss function).

🎲 Inverted Dropout
a_drop = (m · a) / (1 - p)
⚖️ L2 Weight Decay
L_total = L_data + (λ/2)·Σw²

Inverted Dropout Scaling

Why divide by (1 − p) during training?

So that the expected sum of activations remains identical between training and testing! At test time, no scaling is needed.
